Build Safety Plan for the JP-60 Jerk Pit

The hazards of this specific build, the OSHA general-industry standards that apply, and the working tools to run it: a job hazard analysis for all 33 steps, a hot-work permit, a pre-shift checklist, a PPE hazard assessment and a crew training matrix.

How to use this page. It supports your written safety programs; it does not replace them or professional advice. Citations are to 29 CFR Part 1910 (general industry), which covers private-sector shops in Illinois through federal OSHA. Standards change, so check the current text at osha.gov and your customer’s site requirements. Several standards (respiratory protection, HazCom, hearing conservation, hexavalent chromium) require written programs, training and records that must exist in the shop itself.

Shop rules for this build

Non-negotiable. Anyone can stop the job for a safety concern, and nobody is blamed for stopping it.

  1. No galvanized, zinc-plated or cadmium-plated parts anywhere on the pit. Heated zinc causes metal fume fever and contaminates food. (1910.252(c))
  2. No hot work without a posted permit, combustibles cleared 35 ft or shielded, an extinguisher in reach and a named fire watch who stays 30 minutes after the last weld. (1910.252(a))
  3. Grinder guards stay on. Wheels are rated at or above the tool’s RPM and are inspected and ring-tested before mounting. (1910.215, 1910.243)
  4. Plate and finished assemblies move by hoist or forklift on rated rigging. Nobody stands under a suspended load. (1910.184, 1910.179, 1910.178)
  5. Fume extraction runs whenever anyone welds. Stainless gets local exhaust and a fit-tested respirator under a written program. (1910.134, 1910.1026)
  6. Charcoal fires happen outdoors only, at least 10 ft from structures. Carbon monoxide kills without warning. (1910.1000)
  7. Treat all fresh-cut and fresh-welded steel as hot. Mark it HOT and set it on steel, not wood. (1910.132, 1910.138)
  8. Nobody does hot work alone. Nobody operates a machine they have not been trained on. (1910.212, 1910.217)
  9. Lock out the machine before clearing a jam or servicing it. (1910.147)
  10. Keep aisles, exits and extinguisher access clear every shift. (1910.37, 1910.176)

      If something goes wrong

      First response

      Serious injury or fire you can’t control
      Call 911. Evacuate and meet at the assembly point. Shut off power and gas only if it is safe.
      Small fire
      Use the extinguisher only if you have a clear exit behind you. Pull, aim at the base, squeeze, sweep.
      Burn
      Cool under cool running water for 20 minutes. No ice. Get medical care for anything larger than a coin or that blisters.
      Something in the eye
      Flush at the eyewash for 15 minutes. Don’t rub. Get medical care.
      Arc flash (gritty, painful eyes hours later)
      See a medical provider.
      Chemical on skin or eyes (pickling paste, solvents)
      Flush 15 minutes, bring the SDS to the clinic.
      Crush or cut
      Stop bleeding with direct pressure, call 911 for severe injury, lock out the machine.
      Feeling sick after welding
      Fever, chills or a metallic taste can be metal fume fever. Stop work, get fresh air, report it, get checked.
      CO symptoms during burn-in (headache, dizziness, nausea)
      Move everyone to fresh air and call 911.

      Emergency action plan checklist (1910.38)

      • How to report a fire or emergency, and the shop address for 911: 70 E 63rd Street, Chicago, IL 60637
      • Evacuation routes and an assembly point, posted and walked
      • Headcount procedure and who accounts for visitors
      • Who shuts down critical equipment and gas before leaving
      • Rescue and medical duties, and who is first-aid trained
      • Contacts for questions about the plan
      • Eyewash tested weekly; first-aid kit stocked; extinguishers inspected monthly
      • After any incident: record it, investigate causes, fix the cause, record on the OSHA log if it is recordable

      Safety built into the pit for the customer

      What leaves the shop must be safe to use. The owner’s guide and labels carry these controls to the customer. Labels should follow ANSI Z535.4 signal-word and format conventions.

      Built-in controls

      • Counterbalanced lids with stops, so they don’t slam or flip over
      • Handles on standoffs, at least 3″ off hot steel
      • Locking casters and a push-tested, stable footprint
      • Stainless food-contact grates; high-heat paint on the exterior only
      • Rounded edges and corners, no snag points
      • Ash cleanout and drain that work with a gloved hand

      Labels and owner’s guide

      • HOT SURFACE and OUTDOOR USE ONLY with a carbon monoxide warning
      • Keep clear of combustibles; minimum clearances stated
      • Serial plate with model, date and weight
      • Lighting, damper control, tray heights, ash removal, cleaning, re-seasoning
      • Recommend an ABC extinguisher kept at the pit
      • Remind the customer to confirm health department and fire marshal requirements before vending
      • Load-out: strap through the stand frame, never the lids or shelves; casters locked; wheels chocked
